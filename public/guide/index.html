Sign-in and consent

A parent picks a language, enters their number and types the code. SMS is mocked, so the code shows on screen in the demo. Most students are minors, so nothing is shown until a parent agrees, as the DPDP Act 2023 needs.

Parent home

The brief's test: understand your dues in five seconds. So this screen has the amount, the due date and a pay button, and nothing more.

Paying

Each instalment has its own signed pay link, which reminders carry. The test gateway sends a signed webhook, and only that marks anything paid. The browser is never trusted to say it paid.

What happensResult
Exact balance, or lessApplied. Less is a part payment
More than the balanceGoes to the accounts queue, marks nothing paid

Instalments, progress and refunds

Everything else sits one link behind home. Attendance and scores are plain figures, with no risk wording. A parent never sees a risk flag.

Dues dashboard

One view across all centres, with ageing, a table by batch, the queue and the Tally export. Filter by centre or batch. Students who have left are not counted.

Student ledger

The whole story when a parent calls: plan, discount, instalments, payments, balance, days overdue and any risk band.

Reminders

Sent in the parent's language before, on and after each due date, and they stop the moment an instalment is paid. The balance is checked again at send time, under the same lock a payment takes, so a payment cannot slip in during a send.

Try it: drag the slider to see which reminders have gone out, then mark the instalment paid.

Bulk upload

A CSV per batch, with a template, a preview showing old and new values, and clear errors by line. One bad row saves nothing, and a repeat upload changes nothing. Saving never touches fees, reminders or call tasks.

Call tasks: the dropout-risk flag

Gemini gives the flag from missed instalments, falling attendance and test trends. It only ever creates a call task. It never touches fees and never messages a parent. Counsellors mark false flags, and with too little data it says so and creates no task.

Try it: this is the plain rule that takes over when the AI is unsure. Late fees alone never flag anyone.

The AI layer

The brief says "an AI flag" and asks what happens when it is wrong or unsure. So Gemini gives the flag, code holds the brief's hard lines around it, and it all lives inside the screens staff already use.

PartWhat it doesWhen it is wrong or unsure
The flagGemini reads anonymous records and gives each student a band. On the sample it flagged a student the rule had passedIf it is unsure or fails, the rule decides, and the task says who decided. Too little data is never judged, and late fees alone cannot flag
Batch baselineLearns each batch's normal pattern, so a dip the whole batch shares, like exam week, is not blamed on anyoneUnder 3 students it learns nothing, and the plain rule applies
Learning from outcomesThe Risk Lab proposes better numbers from who left and which flags counsellors called falseA person decides. Under 3 cases it says so
Call guideGemini drafts a guide in the parent's languageA draft with money talk, links or placeholders is thrown away for a plain template
Payer notesGemini reads notes like "doosri kist ka advance", and our code matches them to real studentsIt only narrows a suggestion. Money stays unmatched until a person applies it

Gemini never sees a name, ID, phone number or amount. It gets a made-up id, percentages and an overdue count, and parents are told this in the consent notice. With no key, or if the service fails, every step falls back to plain rules, so nothing breaks.

What sets it apart · 1

Reviewer console

A reviewer cannot wait three weeks to see a reminder stop. So the date moves, and every day runs through the real pipeline, with simulated parents paying and centres sending files.

  • Demo mode only, refused at the lowest level, with a test to prove it.
  • Time only moves forward. Going back would leave reminders sent in the future.
  • Reset replays the term, so the next reviewer gets a clean copy.
What sets it apart · 2

Director scoreboard

The founder-director signs the deal, and the brief says what they are paying for. This page works those measures out from the records.

  • Value over 60 days late counts per rupee, and only instalments due 60 or more days ago. Newer ones would flatter the figure.
  • Month-end shows days to close from the recorded close.
  • Parent calls says "Not tracked yet". The app has no call data, and metrics are never invented.
What sets it apart · 3

Refund desk

Refunds are the brief's hard part and a legal duty: pro-rata, within 10 days of a student leaving. Every refund sits on one list, soonest deadline first.

Try it: the same formula as the product. Try leaving on 12 Oct 2026 with ₹45,000 paid.

What sets it apart · 4

Risk lab

There is no real dropout history to train on, and a model trained on invented data would give a fake accuracy number. So this page tests the fallback rule: it replays the term using only what was known each day, and shows who it caught and who it missed. It can also propose better numbers from what really happened.

The sample is invented, so this shows the tool works, not that the rule predicts dropouts. The real test is Company A's own history.

What sets it apart · 5

Close the books

Money that can be corrected without being erased. Month end adds up the finished Tally file against the ledger, and a month closes only when they agree.
